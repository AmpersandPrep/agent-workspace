# Amber Grant Essays

## Essay 1

Ampersand Prep is my SAT and ACT tutoring practice in McHenry, Illinois. I work with college-prep students, mostly juniors and seniors, and I do it differently than the big prep companies. Instead of drilling and memorizing, my students are taught Cognitive Agency, the ability to direct their thinking and use what they have learned to solve problems.

This was developed from a business-forward point of view. I didn't study education; I was a real estate appraiser. When I did the Pythagorean theorem, it was to measure a bay window, not to get a high score on the test.

This sort of approach works impressively well for the SAT and ACT. I refuse to believe there are "good students and bad test takers." I believe good students can be taught to use what they know.

Typical programs boast of score increases of +110 to 150 on the SAT. My students average +245. Typical programs boast +4 on the ACT. My students average +7.5. Those averages come from students who received my full course.

The honest picture: my biggest challenge is reach, not results. My dream is to let more parents know about what I can help their students achieve.

## Essay 2

What I would do with the money is fund the research that moves the Cognitive Agency Approach from test prep to proof of concept.

The Cognitive Agency Approach is not about the SAT or the ACT necessarily. It is about college readiness. It just so happens that the SAT and the ACT test for that. Cognitive Agency is the same thing weed-out classes at universities test for: the ability to use academics and thinking to solve a problem. It is not theory. It is praxis.

I have a small proof of concept already, about 200 students, and I have shown the focus works on high-performing students, scoring around 1200 and up. Research has shown that SAT scores indicate college readiness more than GPA does, and that preparing for the SAT itself is college prep. What I have not been able to do is test whether the approach applies to students of all levels. I want to understand what students who just use my book will achieve at different starting score levels. I have designed the studies. What I am limited by is time and money. I highly suspect it will hold. I have anecdotal evidence that even students who are not intending to go to college, who are not striving for perfect scores or marks, benefit immensely when they switch to thinking about academics as tools instead of rules.

To test it, I would recruit 60 to 100 students across three starting score bands. Within each band, half would study with the official SAT guide and half would study with my book. Students would study on their own, with a weekly check-in where I answer questions. Each student would take a test before the study and another after it, so I can compare which group improved most, overall and within each band.

Here is the budget for the study, planned at 90 students:

My time directing the study: $10,000. This covers recruiting the students, designing the study, running the weekly check-ins, administering the proctored before and after tests, and scoring and analyzing the results. It is a soft cost, the value of my own labor.

Ethics and compliance: $2,500. That is $2,000 for an independent review of the study design, and $500 for a course in ethical study design.

Books: $1,844.55. Half the students get my book at my cost of $6 a copy, 45 copies for $270. Half get The Official SAT Study Guide at $34.99 a copy, 45 copies for $1,574.55.

Recruitment and testing materials: $655.45, for outreach to reach students scoring below 1200 and for materials for the two proctored test days.

The hard costs total $5,000. With my time, the study budget totals exactly $15,000. If enrollment lands at 60 or 100 students instead of 90, the book line moves, from $1,229.70 up to $2,049.50, and the recruitment line absorbs the difference, so the total stays $15,000.

Separate from this budget, after the study every study student would receive a six-week SAT course from me as a thank you. Students would not pay for that course. I am estimating its value at $500 per student, or $30,000 to $50,000 in teaching value across the study group. The course would run separately from my regular students, on Friday nights or weekends, because the study students cannot simply be folded into my existing classes.

That proof is what I need to market this approach to schools. It is also what would let the approach move beyond test prep, into vocational programs, elementary education, and potentially even adult programs. And it is a hopeful endeavor, instead of the normal doom and gloom that "kids these days aren't being taught anything more than what to memorize."
