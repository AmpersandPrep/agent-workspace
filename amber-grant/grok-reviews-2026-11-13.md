## Review 1 — on the essays with the detailed budget added

Strong concept overall. It fits what WomensNet looks for: a clear, differentiated business, personal origin story, honest challenges, and a specific, purposeful use of the money. Judges score on clarity of purpose, passion/vision/story, market understanding, use of funds, and viability. Your submission hits most of those.

What works well

Differentiation is sharp. Cognitive Agency vs. traditional drill-and-memorize is easy to understand and positions you against the big prep companies. The appraiser background (Pythagorean theorem for bay windows, not test scores) is a memorable personal hook that supports the "praxis over theory" claim.

Results are the hook. The +245 SAT / +7.5 ACT averages versus typical program claims are attention-grabbing. Pairing that with the honest limitation (mostly higher-scoring students, about 200 total) shows self-awareness instead of pure hype.

Use of funds is concrete and tied to the idea. The study design (score bands, official guide control, pre/post tests, weekly check-ins) is specific enough that a judge can picture it. Budgeting exactly to $15k, including ethics review, and noting the free post-study course as a thank-you are thoughtful details.

Vision scales beyond tutoring. Moving from "works on high performers in test prep" to "does it work across levels, and could it apply to vocational/elementary/adult settings" gives the grant a bigger-purpose feel. The "hopeful instead of doom-and-gloom" framing is positive.

Potential judge concerns (concept-level)

Credibility of the averages. +245 is well above typical published prep gains. Judges may wonder about selection effects, baseline scores, or sample composition. The fact that you've mostly worked with 1200+ students is already noted; making that limitation even clearer (and any supporting context you have) reduces the risk that the numbers feel overstated.

Research vs. direct business growth. The stated business challenge in Essay 1 is reach, not results. The $15k is almost entirely for a study (with $10k as your own time). Some judges prefer funds that immediately expand the practice (marketing, systems, capacity). Framing the study as the thing that unlocks broader reach and new markets helps, but it is still one step removed from "this money grows the tutoring business now."

Soft costs dominate. $10k of the budget is the value of your labor. Hard costs are only $5k. That's defensible for a study, but some grant reviewers discount or scrutinize self-labor more heavily than external spend.

Study rigor. It's a reasonable proof-of-concept design, not a formal research study. Budgeting for independent design review is smart and shows awareness; just don't overclaim what it can prove.

Bottom line

The concept is coherent, differentiated, and purpose-driven. The personal story supports the method, the results create interest, and the use of funds is specific rather than vague ("marketing and operations"). The main risks are skepticism about the magnitude of the score gains and the fact that the money goes primarily to research rather than immediate business expansion. Both are manageable if the final version stays precise and grounded.

When you rewrite, prioritize clarity, specificity, and honesty over polish. Those traits align well with how these applications are scored.

## Review 2 — on the updated Essay 2 (full-course results separated from the book-only study)

The updated Essay 2 works better. It now clearly separates the existing full-course results from the lighter, book-focused version you want to test. That distinction was the main missing piece.

What improved

The purpose is sharper: "I want to understand what students who just use my book will achieve at different starting score levels." This directly supports the school-marketing goal. Schools are more likely to adopt a book-plus-light-support model than a full private course, so testing exactly that version makes the study relevant to the expansion you care about.

It no longer risks implying the book alone has already produced the +245 / +7.5 gains. The full-course results stay in Essay 1; this essay is about testing a more scalable format.

The study design (score bands, official-guide control, self-study + weekly check-ins, pre/post) matches the narrower claim. You're isolating the book's contribution rather than re-testing the full course.

Remaining concept notes

The link to schools is still mostly implicit. A single sentence tying the study results to school outreach (e.g., "These results would give me the evidence I need to approach schools with a practical, lower-intensity version of the approach") would make the business case more obvious to a judge reading only these two essays.

Soft costs ($10k of your time) still dominate. That is reasonable for a founder-run study, but the hard-cost total ($5k) is modest. Judges who prefer money spent on external growth activities may still note this; the school-marketing payoff is the best counter-argument you have.

The free six-week course remains a nice participant benefit and a soft entry point, but it is correctly kept outside the $15k budget.

Overall, the concept is now cleaner and more defensible. The study is positioned as a deliberate next step to generate the specific evidence needed for a new channel (schools), using a lighter delivery model. That is a coherent use-of-funds story for the Amber Grant.
